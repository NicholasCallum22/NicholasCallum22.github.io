## The problem

Pitti Engineering builds components for EV traction motors and powertrains. I worked on liquid-cooled motors: the stator, rotor, housing and the parts that keep coolant where it belongs. My work covered three problems: coolant-jacket leaks in hold testing, subassembly mass, and vibration in a rotating assembly.

## What I did

**Coolant-jacket sealing.** In 2.5 bar gauge hydrostatic hold tests, 11 of 50 baseline stator housings failed. I redesigned the axial O-ring gland in SolidWorks: groove width, an 18% to 22% crush ratio and Ra ≤ 1.6 µm surface-finish callouts. I also tightened the positional GD&T on the counterbore.

**Mass and power density.** I revised the stator back-iron and tooth-slot geometry and simplified the end-shield and winding-coil enclosure using a DFA-led rabbet and fastener-boss layout. Steady-state and transient thermal FEA confirmed the end-winding hot spots stayed below the Class H limit of 180 °C.

**Laminations.** I redesigned stator and rotor lamination stacks in SolidWorks and CATIA V5 with FEA support, changing the stack geometry to reduce stamping waste.

**Noise and vibration.** During continuous endurance testing I assessed the vibration of a rotating assembly and tightened the journal-bearing clearances from 0.05 mm to 0.03 mm to raise dynamic stiffness.

## Results

| Result | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Hold-test failure rate down from 22% (11 of 50) to 4% (2 of 50) in pre-series testing: 18 percentage points | Test data |
| Gravimetric power density up 9% (kW/kg), from about 8.3% lower subassembly mass at unchanged rated torque and power | Calculation |
| Stamping material waste down 15% | Reported |
| Operational NVH down 9 dB | Workplace result |

## Limits

The sealing result comes from two sets of 50 units, so it is a small sample. The power-density figure is a mechanical, mass-based calculation, not an electromagnetic simulation, and it is not an efficiency figure. Motor efficiency was never quantified, so I don't quote a number for it.
